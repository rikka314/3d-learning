## Problem

Visually plausible outputs are often not deployable in game engines, simulators or digital-twin systems because topology, UVs, PBR channels, rigs, skinning and physical layout are missing or unreliable.

## Core idea

Re-index the literature by the asset-production lifecycle (data, geometry, topology, UV, PBR, rigging and scene assembly), crossed with object, character and scene tiers.

## Claimed contributions

- A production-pipeline taxonomy rather than an algorithm-only taxonomy.
- Consolidation of usability metrics and protocols.
- Identification of assetization and data annotation as the main practical bottlenecks.

## Method

Survey and pipeline analysis; no new generator is introduced.

## Experimental setup
- Datasets/simulators/robots: Not applicable.
- Baselines: Cross-paper survey comparisons.
- Metrics: Geometric fidelity, appearance, usability and scene-level physical plausibility are reviewed; no single new metric suite is proposed.
- Ablations: not found.
- Sim-only, real-robot, or mixed: not applicable.

## Main evidence
- What the paper directly supports: Production-ready generation requires engine-level structure beyond appearance [Paper Abstract, §§3–8].
- What remains unsupported or weak: The survey does not validate a single end-to-end pipeline or establish a universal production threshold.

## Limitations and failure cases

Solutions remain fragmented by stage; web-scale data lack topology, PBR and rig annotations; generated meshes can still require retopology and manual cleanup [Paper Conclusion].

## What is reusable

Use its lifecycle as an audit checklist for anatomical assets: watertightness, editable parts, UV/material channels, deformation support and simulation constraints.

## What is questionable

The “production-ready” label is application-dependent; game, robotics and clinical visualization impose different tolerances and are not directly interchangeable.

## Relation to our project

Provides the clearest bridge from 3D generation to deployable patient-specific assets, but offers no CT/MRI/ultrasound evidence and must not be treated as clinical guidance.

## Citation notes
- Safe claims this paper can support: The asset-production lifecycle and the identified assetization/data gaps (direct).
- Claims this paper should not be used to support: That any surveyed method is clinically deployable or physically validated (unsupported).
