- Venue/year: arXiv preprint, 2025
- DOI/arXiv/OpenReview: arXiv:2503.21745v3

## Problem

Automatic 3D metrics do not reliably track human judgments, and the field lacks a large preference dataset covering both text-to-3D and image-to-3D.

## Core idea

Build 3DGen-Arena to collect pairwise and absolute human judgments, then train 3DGen-Score (CLIP-based) and 3DGen-Eval (MLLM-based) as complementary evaluators.

## Claimed contributions

- 11,200 generated assets from 19 models.
- More than 68,000 preference votes / about 56,000 score labels (the paper reports both counts in different summaries).
- Five criteria: geometry plausibility, geometry details, texture quality, geometry-texture coherence and prompt-asset alignment.

## Method

Prompt curation, model battle comparisons, public/expert annotation, CLIP reward-model fine-tuning and MLLM evaluation.

## Experimental setup
- Datasets/simulators/robots: Curated text/image prompts; generated 3D assets from 9 text-to-3D and 13 image-to-3D systems.
- Baselines: CLIP-Score, aesthetic and other existing 3D metrics.
- Metrics: Pairwise human win rate, five human criteria, rank correlation and evaluator accuracy.
- Ablations: Evaluator/model and criterion analyses are reported; exact ablation table details should be checked in the PDF.
- Sim-only, real-robot, or mixed: Synthetic/generated assets; no robot experiment.

## Main evidence
- What the paper directly supports: 3DGen-Score correlates better with human rankings than the compared automatic metrics on the collected benchmark [Paper §§3–5, Conclusion].
- What remains unsupported or weak: Correlation is benchmark- and annotation-distribution-dependent; it is not proof of geometric correctness or physical validity.

## Limitations and failure cases

Human preference can contain annotator and model-selection bias; generated assets inherit source licenses; evaluator errors remain and the authors call for stronger architectures [Paper Conclusion and Data Availability].

## What is reusable

Adopt the multi-criterion annotation design when evaluating anatomy: separate geometry, texture/material, cross-view coherence and condition alignment instead of one aggregate score.

## What is questionable

A preference-aligned score may reward visual plausibility while missing topology, watertightness, correspondence or clinical semantics.

## Relation to our project

Useful as a perceptual layer for 3D anatomical generation, but it must be paired with geometric and medical metrics (surface distance, topology, segmentation overlap and registration error).

## Citation notes
- Safe claims this paper can support: Dataset scale, five criteria and human-alignment experiments (direct).
- Claims this paper should not be used to support: Clinical correctness, physical simulation accuracy or universal metric validity (unsupported).
