## Problem

3D generation spans heterogeneous representations and conditioning modes, but prior work lacked a common taxonomy and a consolidated account of quality, controllability and efficiency bottlenecks.

## Core idea

Organize the field into 3D-native generation, 2D-prior-based lifting, and hybrid methods, then compare their representations, training signals, applications and open problems.

## Claimed contributions

- A taxonomy covering roughly 60 papers.
- A cross-method discussion of quality, controllability, efficiency and data limitations.
- An open project page collecting 3D-generation resources.

## Method

This is a literature survey rather than a new model. The analysis contrasts native 3D latent/voxel/mesh methods with SDS-style 2D priors and hybrid pipelines.

## Main evidence
- What the paper directly supports: The taxonomy and the cited method-by-method synthesis [Paper Abstract, §§2–5].
- What remains unsupported or weak: It does not provide a meta-analysis with a common dataset, metric, or statistical aggregation across papers.

## Limitations and failure cases

The survey identifies poor mesh compactness/topology, baked lighting and weak material modeling, ambiguous control, scarce high-quality 3D data, and high cost as unresolved issues [Paper §6]. Clinical validity is not studied.

## What is reusable

Use the three-way taxonomy and the representation/conditioning comparison as a reading map; use the open-problem list to structure a research agenda.

## What is questionable

Cross-paper quality statements are not directly comparable because training data, renderers, prompts and metrics differ; “state of the art” claims in cited papers should be rechecked in their original sources.

## Relation to our project

Useful as the top-level map for connecting patient-specific reconstruction, structured anatomical assets and evaluation. It is background only and not evidence for medical performance.

## Citation notes
- Safe claims this paper can support: The three-category taxonomy and the survey's stated open challenges (direct).
- Claims this paper should not be used to support: Any numerical ranking, clinical safety claim, or superiority of one method on a shared benchmark (unsupported).
